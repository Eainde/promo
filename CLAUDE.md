## ECDF Framework (Evaluation Criteria)

### 4 Behaviours (HOW you deliver)
1. **Thinks** — intellectual curiosity, creates solutions, conceptual agility
2. **Engages** — builds relationships/trust, collaborates, promotes capability
3. **Influences** — influences others, communicates with impact
4. **Achieves** — drives outcomes, continuous improvement, quality culture

### 4 Technical Capabilities (WHAT you deliver)
1. **Designs** — architecture, design thinking, capacity mgmt, API/data modelling
2. **Delivers** — engineering craftsmanship (BDD, coding, building solutions)
3. **Operates** — monitoring, incident mgmt, SRE, chaos engineering
4. **Controls** — information security

### 5 Competency Levels
1. Developing → 2. Competent → 3. Skilled → 4. Expert → 5. Principal

### VP/Senior Engineer Target
- Most dimensions at **Level 3 (Skilled)**
- Deep specialism + growing behavioural breadth (T-shaped)
- At senior roles, **behaviours must increase faster than technical skills**

## Critical Feedback to Address (from 2025 rejection)
1. **Business language** — no jargon/acronyms, explain impact in simple business terms
2. **Leadership & cooperation** — show partnership, not just technical ability
3. **Firmwide contribution** — was weak last time, must be prominent
4. **Sponsors** — get 1 from tech + 1 outside CLM
5. **Solution design ownership** — show individual contribution to design decisions
6. **D4 (Director) involvement** — demonstrate engagement with senior leadership
7. **PPT readability** — bigger text, no wall-of-text. ⚠️ **2026 constraint: ENG track = 1-page PPT only** — must fit while staying readable
8. **Always tie back to business benefit** — every technical example must show business value

## Writing Guidelines
- Be extremely concise, sacrifice grammar for concision
- Speech must be exactly 7 minutes (roughly 900-1000 words)
- Use storytelling with before/after narratives, not bullet-point lists
- Every technical achievement must be translated to business impact in plain English
- Avoid acronyms — spell out or explain in simple terms when first used
- **Never use em-dashes, en-dashes, or semicolons** in presentations or speeches. Use commas, periods, or "and" instead. They look AI-generated.
